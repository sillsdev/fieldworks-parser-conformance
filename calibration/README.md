# Calibration grammars

**CALIBRATION ONLY: never run in conformance or engine test runs.** These words are slow on purpose (a word can
cost 10G, a grammar can hold 50,000 entries). They are for cost calibration under a profiler, with the cap or
flags this file names. No engine, test suite or CI job may enumerate `calibration/`; `scripts/check_calib_a.py`
fails if a calibration path reaches a manifest or a consumer-enumerated category (`languages/`, `edge-cases/`,
`stress/edge-cases/`).

These grammars are the standing calibration set for parser-work cost weights (PanGloss #240). They are
synthetic: each one scales a mechanism the existing suite only touches lightly (the survey found a median
per-grammar maximum cost of 0.25M, tiny lexicons, nothing at work-cap scale and no wasted-search words).

They are **not conformance fixtures**. They live outside `languages/`, `edge-cases/` and `stress/`, outside both
manifests, and `docs/grammars.md` does not list them. Each `words.yaml` starts with `# kind: calibration`, keeps
the repository's rule (only what FieldWorks can produce, section 1 of `AGENTS.md`) and carries
`fieldworks_producible: true`, a claim read from `HCLoader.cs`; no family has a FieldWorks witness.

Each family directory holds:

| File | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `grammar.xml` | generated by `scripts/calib_a_gen.py`; byte-identical on every run |
| `words.txt`, `words.yaml` | the selected words and the C# oracle's analyses for them; each word has a note, `parses` carry the oracle's signature and the rule ids it traced |
| `ladder.tsv` | the measured status, work steps and cost of every selected word, and the rung it was picked for |
| `cap-words.work-cap-200000.txt`, `.yaml` | `cal-work-cap` only: words that stop at the reduced work cap; the name carries the cap, run them only with `--work-cap 200000`; no analyses are recorded |
| `budget_ms: 600000` | in `words.yaml`; guards against a hang only |

## Families added by batch A (scale)

Costs are the `cost` column of `pangloss batch --cost-column --threads 1` on the 0.19.4 release binary (the
unit the cost model uses; one work step is about 700 to 900 cost). `span` is the maximum over the minimum cost
of the family. Words are 5 to 40 characters.

| family | what grows | words | no-parse | min cost | max cost | span | word length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cal-large-lexicon` | 5000 roots, 10 optional suffix slots, 60 rules | 25 | 6 | 0.11M | 28.0M | 261x | 7-28 |
| `cal-allomorph-explosion` | 36 suffix rules, 64 allomorphs each, 200 roots with a conditioned second allomorph | 20 | 8 | 0.90M | 53.2M | 59x | 5-40 |
| `cal-homophone-ambiguity` | 30 root forms shared by 4 nouns and 2 verbs, 3 homophonous suffix rules per slot | 25 | 8 | 0.32M | 87.7M | 278x | 6-18 |
| `cal-compounding-large` | 1500 roots, two endocompound rules | 20 | 8 | 0.43M | 27.6M | 65x | 7-34 |
| `cal-work-cap` | 28 optional affix rules that insert one letter | 6 + 2 cap | 3 | 0.94M | 152.0M | 162x | 5-8 |
| `cal-prefilter-scale` | 700 roots, 10 suffix rules, 640 words | 640 | 198 | 0.08M | 9.3M | 117x | 6-30 |

Batch B adds its rows to this table.

The ladder aim is words near 1x, 4x, 16x, 64x and 256x of the cheapest word. A word joins a rung only within
2.5x of the rung's target, so a rung with no word is empty rather than filled with a nearer one. Costs per rung:

| family | 1x | 4x | 16x | 64x | 256x |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cal-large-lexicon` | 0.11M | 0.31-0.32M | 1.7M | 6.3-7.1M | 26-28M |
| `cal-allomorph-explosion` | 0.90-0.92M | 3.5-3.6M | 14.3-14.4M | 47-53M | empty (no word above 59x) |
| `cal-homophone-ambiguity` | 0.32-0.42M | 1.1-1.9M | 4.9-5.7M | 19-20M | 74-88M |
| `cal-compounding-large` | 0.43-0.60M | 1.7-1.8M | 6.8M | 26-28M | empty (no word above 65x) |
| `cal-work-cap` | 0.94M, 2.3M | 7.5M, 8.2M | empty | 60M, 84M | 143M, 152M (both at the cap) |
| `cal-prefilter-scale` | 0.08M | 0.31-0.32M | 1.2M | 5.0-5.3M | 9.1-9.3M (top is 117x) |

The two empty rungs are a limit of the grammar at 40 characters, not a selection choice. The no-parse words are
the wasted-search words: they fail only after the engine has explored the whole space, and on equal length they
cost several times more than a parsing word.

## The lexicon-size ladder

Does parser cost track instructions linearly as one driver grows? `cal-lexsize-1k`, `-5k`, `-10k` and `-50k` answer it
for the lexicon: the same 12 suffix rules (4 optional slots, `LoadAffixTemplate`, `LoadInflAffixProcessRule`,
`LoadFormAffixProcessAllomorph`) and the same 16 words (12 parsing, 4 wasted-search, 7-14 characters) over lexicons
of 1,000, 5,000, 10,000 and 50,000 generated roots. Each smaller lexicon is a prefix of the next, the oracle's
analyses of every word are identical in all four, and the self-check passes 4 of 4. Constructs are those of
`cal-large-lexicon`; no phonological rule.

| grammar | grammar.xml | load ms | morpher ms | peak RSS | Ir, 16 words | Ir, 1 word | Ir of the other 15 words | cost, 16 words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cal-lexsize-1k` | 0.23 MB, committed | 10.9 | 1.6 | 11.5 MB | 71.0M | 59.1M | 11.9M | 11.15M |
| `cal-lexsize-5k` | 1.1 MB, committed | 35.2 | 9.2 | 25.1 MB | 277.1M | 265.3M | 11.8M | 11.30M |
| `cal-lexsize-10k` | 2.2 MB, committed | 78.3 | 15.3 | 43.8 MB | 535.5M | 523.7M | 11.8M | 11.34M |
| `cal-lexsize-50k` | 11.2 MB, **not committed** | 514.4 | 198.5 | 195.3 MB | 2630.0M | 2618.0M | 12.0M | 11.44M |

Measured on the 0.19.4 release binary, `--threads 1`; Ir is the callgrind total of the whole process, so it includes
grammar load. Finding: parse cost is flat in lexicon size (cost +2.6% and parse Ir +1% from 1k to 50k) while load
grows linearly, about 52,000 instructions per entry; at 50k, load is 99.5% of the instructions of a 16-word batch.
A calibration that counts whole-process instructions at this size measures the loader, not the parser.

**The 50k grammar is 11.2 MB, over the 10 MB limit, so it is not committed.** `cal-lexsize-50k/` holds `words.yaml`,
`words.txt`, `ladder.tsv` and `grammar.sha256` (SHA-256 of the exact LF text the generator writes);
`scripts/check_calib_a.py` regenerates it and fails on a different hash, or if a `grammar.xml` over 10 MB is
committed. To get the file: `python3 scripts/calib_a_gen.py lexsize PANGLOSS HC_DLL SCRATCH` writes it as
`SCRATCH/cal-lexsize-50k.grammar.xml` (or call `fam_lexsize(50000)["xml"]`). The other three are committed.

## What each family exercises, and the loader function behind it

Every construct is one the suite already uses (the stress fixtures use most; `CompoundingRule` and a right `RequiredEnvironments` appear only in conformance fixtures). Function names are in `HCLoader.cs`
(`Src/LexText/ParserCore/HCLoader.cs`); line numbers are the ones the writable-shapes census gives.

| family | constructs | `HCLoader.cs` |
|---|---|---|
| `cal-large-lexicon` | root entries with one allomorph; one affix template of optional slots; slot rules with a lone suffix form | `LoadLexEntry` 745-760, `LoadRootAllomorph` 858-894, `LoadAffixTemplate` 1722-1855, `LoadInflAffixProcessRule` 1025, `LoadFormAffixProcessAllomorph` 1626 |
| `cal-allomorph-explosion` | the same, plus 64 allomorphs per rule whose stem context sits in the input pattern, and a root allomorph with a right `RequiredEnvironments` | the above, plus `LoadRootAllomorph` 862-876 |
| `cal-homophone-ambiguity` | several entries per surface form; one template per part of speech; rules with identical forms | `LoadLexEntry`, `LoadAffixTemplate` (`requiredPartsOfSpeech` 1731-1733), `LoadInflAffixProcessRule` |
| `cal-compounding-large` | two endocentric compounding rules (noun+noun, verb+noun), templates on both parts of speech | `LoadEndoCompoundingRule` 1891-1969, `LoadInflAffixProcessRule` |
| `cal-work-cap` | unslotted (unclassified) affix rules, each a lone prefix or suffix form, with a required part of speech | `LoadUnclassifiedAffixProcessRule` 1057, `LoadFormAffixProcessAllomorph` 1626-1653 |
| `cal-prefilter-scale` | as `cal-large-lexicon`, with a template of 5 slots | as `cal-large-lexicon` |

No family uses a phonological rule, a feature system or a stratum beyond `Main`, so a rule list in `words.yaml`
is exact. Compounding stays at the parser's default of two roots (`MaxRoots`, `PROTOCOL.md` section 8): the batch
command takes no morpher block, so a three-root word would measure only the refusal.

## Expected analyses and oracle status

Every analysis in a `words.yaml` is the C# oracle's: `hc.dll batch` from the build named `aab4c598` in
`PROVENANCE.md` (`# oracle-provenance:` in each header). Each fixture also passes the harness self-check of
`hc-conformance` (`--fixtures <tree holding the six directories under edge-cases/> --include-pathological`: 6 of 6
pass, which checks every declared rule list against the oracle's trace). PanGloss 0.19.4 returns the same
signatures for every word in every `words.txt` (0 mismatches over 746 words). `cal-work-cap` records only the
words below the cap in `words.yaml`; the oracle has no work cap, so a word that stops at the cap gets no analyses.

## The work cap

`cal-work-cap` is the deep-optional shape widened (`edge-cases/deep-optional-affix-nesting` at scale): 20
optional prefix rules that all insert `x` and 8 optional suffix rules that all insert `y`, over four verb
roots. A word `x^a root y^b` whose stem is not in the lexicon is searched over every subset of the rules that
could have fired, which grows as C(20,a) times C(8,b).

- **Reduced cap for callgrind: `--work-cap 200000`.** At this cap `cap-words.work-cap-200000.txt` ends in status `CAP`, at
  200,000 work steps plus the confirmation allowance (at most 212,500 steps), about 150M cost each, and is
  deterministic. The six words in `words.txt` finish below the cap. Run both files at the same cap. `xxmiloy`
  is a valid word (`x*2 milo y*1`) and still stops: it parses under a larger cap, so it pins the cap, not
  the grammar.
- **Default cap (5,000,000,000 steps): measured on the two shipped cap words.** Each ran alone under
  `ulimit -v 16000000` and `timeout 600`, on the 0.19.4 release binary, after `stile admit`:

  | word | outcome | work steps | cost | analyses | wall | peak RSS |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `xxmiloy` | ok | 1,775,524 | 1.57G | 9120 (duplicates included) | 0.18 s | 26.6 MB |
  | `xxxmilmy` | ok, no analysis | 14,338,007 | 9.81G | none | 1.06 s | 165 MB |

  Neither was killed. So `cap-words.work-cap-200000.txt` is safe to run at the default cap on a small host, but
  one word costs up to 9.8G, about 60x a reduced-cap word, so never feed it to callgrind without the flag. The raw
  output is kept outside this repository for a PanGloss issue on per-word memory. A different word of the
  same grammar (`x^20 milm y^6`, not shipped) ran 6 min 58 s and peaked at 25.9 GB without writing a row: the
  family is the memory hazard, the two shipped words are not.
- `MEMCAP` is a different ceiling (the 256 MiB capture state, `--capture-cap-mib`); no word in this set is expected to reach it.

## The prefilter family

`cal-prefilter-scale` has 640 words so a batch passes the 512-word precondition of `--prefilter auto`.

- `--prefilter auto`: the gate runs (64 warm-up words) and **declines**:
  `admission=off(construction-budget:10857 of 10544 units)`. Its admission build needs 19,158 units, 1.8 times the
  budget, which is one 64th of the projected plain cost.
- `--prefilter on`: the filter engages and shows its work: `restricted=438 rejected=202 admit_steps=76260
  tiled_steps=6976 filter_cost=6586920`, with `outcome_cost=465582018` against `748103639` plain. The 202 rejected
  words are the no-parse words.
- To make `auto` admit, the plain cost of the list would have to roughly double (about 1.5G), which this
  set does not spend. Calibrators of the prefilter weights should pass `--prefilter on` and read the
  `PREFILTER-TOTAL` line; `--prefilter auto` is kept as a regression check that the gate still declines.
- A grammar with fewer rules builds a cheaper filter but its words stop costing enough: a 3-rule variant
  passed the budget and was then declined at calibration because the filter saved nothing.

## Running them

`cal-work-cap`'s cap words must always run with `--work-cap 200000`; the file name says so. Without the flag a cap word costs 1.6G to 9.8G.

The budget is one callgrind run of every list. Measured with `valgrind --tool=callgrind` on the 0.19.4 release
binary, `--threads 1`, after `stile admit --jobs 4`, under `nice -n 10 ionice -c 3`:

| family | cost | wall | instructions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cal-large-lexicon` | 181M | 3.3 s | 0.51G |
| `cal-allomorph-explosion` | 349M | 8.4 s | 1.68G |
| `cal-homophone-ambiguity` | 540M | 4.1 s | 0.71G |
| `cal-compounding-large` | 179M | 2.0 s | 0.30G |
| `cal-work-cap` (both files, cap 200000) | 457M | 4.6 s | 0.62G |
| `cal-prefilter-scale` (`--prefilter auto`) | 753M | 9.8 s | 1.00G |
| total | 2.46G | 32 s | 4.8G |

If a run needs the earlier planning figure of 3 to 4 minutes per G of cost, the total is 7 to 10 minutes; the
measured rate is far lower, so the set has room to grow.

## Regenerating

```
python3 scripts/calib_a_gen.py grammars x SCRATCH             # grammar.xml, candidate words to SCRATCH
python3 scripts/calib_a_gen.py select PANGLOSS SCRATCH [FAM]  # measure, pick the ladder, write words.txt, ladder.tsv
python3 scripts/calib_a_gen.py yaml HC_DLL SCRATCH [FAM]      # oracle batch, write words.yaml, cap-words.yaml
python3 scripts/calib_a_gen.py lexsize PANGLOSS HC_DLL SCRATCH  # the four lexicon-size grammars, words, ladder.tsv, hashes
python3 scripts/calib_a_gen.py table x SCRATCH                # the tables above
python3 scripts/check_calib_a.py                              # shape check (also regenerates and compares each grammar)
```

Every random choice has a fixed seed, so the grammars regenerate byte for byte. The selection depends on the
engine build that measured it (0.19.4); a new build changes `ladder.tsv`, not the words, until `select` is run.
